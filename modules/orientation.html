What the data does to it

The left panel is the model, the answer both seismic sections are trying to show. A sand of constant thickness lies between layers of the rock chosen in the panel, with a thin contrasting bed above it and a tight sandstone bed below. When the surrounding rock is itself tight sandstone, that lower bed has no contrast and gives no reflection. The sand’s porosity rises from 12% at the left of the line to 33% at the right, and the color of the sand shows its pore fluid.

On the stack section each boundary of the sand appears as a wavelet, a trough or a peak with sidelobes above and below it. The brightness of each band combines the impedance contrast at that boundary with the thickness of the bed, and the interior of the sand sits near a zero crossing.

On the DQ section the same sand appears as one filled body between its two boundaries. Higher porosity lowers the sand’s density and velocity, so its impedance falls. Under the default tight sandstone that widens the contrast with the rock above, and the DQ color strengthens from left to right with the porosity in the model. Under a soft shale the sand’s impedance approaches the shale’s as porosity rises, so the contrast and the DQ value weaken instead. The DQ value responds to the contrast between rocks, not to porosity alone. Changing the pore fluid changes the color while the outline of the body stays in place.

Both sections are computed from the same two partial stacks. The DQ section does not add information to them; it assigns a value to every sample inside the sand, where the stack section is close to zero (Bedle et al., 2025a). The modules that follow build that step by step.
